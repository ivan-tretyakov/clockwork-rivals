# Clockwork-only prototype playtest

Use rules **0.3.0** and record the configuration. Keep card values fixed within a comparison. Living Frontier is outside this prototype. See [iteration notes](ITERATION-03.md).

1. Teach the station sequence, free acquisition versus paid installation, full inputs before refunds, and public race versus final objective scoring.
2. Play a full match with two people, then rematch with swapped initiative. Defaults: target 18, objective bonus 3, shared coal 3; no round cutoff. Choose one of two objectives before the markets open.
3. Export **Game menu → Export public match report** and **Playtest notes**. Reports omit private hands, draws, discards and unused objectives and cannot resume games. Browser autosave resumes local games; online sessions persist on the server. Collect private objective feedback separately after the match.
4. Record elapsed active time, waiting/handling time, finishing round, first investment and its first useful return, how often the completed combination operated, and voluntary rematch interest.

Ask which install-versus-deliver decision was difficult, which objective changed a decision, and whether the losing player has another plan to try. Track purchases and replacements by definition, especially Gear Cutter openings. Record disabled stations, wasted output, fuel shortages and unavailable specialist commissions. Distinguish a missed commission from inability to produce its recipe.

Check whether Energy and Conversion investments compete with direct gear upgrades, whether objectives are effectively automatic or abandoned, and whether a private hand of three provides useful flexibility. Separate objective selection rate from completion and win rate; account for player experience.

The first human gate remains 4–6 pairs with repeated sessions. Tune prices, recipes and target prestige before expanding the catalogue. Eight to ten rounds and 20–30 minutes are tuning goals, not end conditions or established results. Arithmetic examples and bot games validate only their stated assumptions.
