# Clockwork-only prototype playtest

Use rules 0.2.0 and record the chosen configuration. Keep card values fixed within a comparison. Living Frontier is outside this implementation. See [iteration notes](ITERATION-02.md) for the rules changes and provisional mixed-resource recipes.

1. Play a teaching game in Pass & play or with the Automaton. Then let two people play a private room without coaching.
2. Reverse starting initiative using Rematch. Try at least two deals. Use the local seed input when reproducing a confusing position.
3. At the end, download **Game menu → Export public match report** and **Playtest notes**. Public reports exclude private cards and cannot restore a match. Browser autosave resumes local games. Notes stay local until the player chooses to share the downloaded file.
4. Record elapsed active play time, rounds, winner, unspent resources, skipped actions, rule questions, and whether both players choose another game. The journal includes all accepted gameplay and automatic transitions.

Ask: Which combination felt satisfying? Which rival move changed your plan? When did the winner feel decided? Which machine felt weak? What would you try in a rematch?

For this iteration, also record which decision the objective changed, the reason for production-plan edits, actual fuel shortages and cap waste, and how often a working combination ran before the match ended. Ask players privately about unchosen offers; never put them in opponent-visible logs. Compare choose-two versus random-one, or mixed versus classic commissions, one setting at a time. Separate objective choice/completion frequency from win rate and account for experience. Keep the default 10 public prestige, eight rounds and three shared coal until human evidence supports a pacing change.

The initial human gate remains 4–6 pairs, observing completion without correcting game state and whether players voluntarily rematch. Automated simulations and browser checks validate operation, not strategic balance or the target duration.
